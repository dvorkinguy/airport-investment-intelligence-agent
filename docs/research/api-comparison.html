What each one is actually good at, and where it breaks

FAA Data Portal

Government · CC0 public domain

The source of truth everyone else resells. Split across several gateways with very different maturity: APRA and the airport-status feeds are open and modern, NOTAM and pilot-records sit behind approval workflows.

BTS TranStats

Government · no API, bulk ZIP only

Thirty-nine years of flight-level microdata with regulator-grade provenance. There is no API at all — the machine path is an open directory of pre-zipped CSVs, and the documentation makes up for it with unusually precise field definitions.

03

Quick integration test

Copy-paste calls to verify each source in under a minute

FAA — national airport status no key

# live XML, sub-minute freshness
curl https://nasstatus.faa.gov/api/\
airport-status-information
# ASWS, JSON or XML
curl https://external-api.faa.gov/api/\
airport/delays

Delay endpoints cover a fixed list of 41 major airports. APRA chart downloads at external-api.faa.gov/apra are also unauthenticated.

BTS — bulk on-time download no key

# one month of flight-level records
wget https://transtats.bts.gov/PREZIP/\
On_Time_Reporting_Carrier_On_Time_\
Performance_1987_present_2026_5.zip

There is no API. Browse the open directory for T-100, DB1B and delay-cause archives. Watch for inconsistent filenames — some months wrap the year range in parentheses.

AviationStack — live flight status key required

curl "https://api.aviationstack.com/v1/flights\
?access_key=YOUR_KEY&flight_iata=BA2490"

Counts against 100 free calls per month. /timetable and /flightsfuture are locked on free and rate-limited to 1 req/min. Errors return JSON and do not consume quota.

AviationAPI — charts and METAR no key

# approach plates for SFO
curl "https://api.aviationapi.com/v1/charts\
?apt=KSFO&group=6"
# airport metadata, ~40 fields
curl "https://api.aviationapi.com/v1/airports\
?apt=KSFO"

Group codes: 1 all · 2 airport diagram · 3 general · 4 departures · 5 arrivals · 6 approaches · 7 everything but general.

PreflightAPI — METAR key required

curl -H "Ocp-Apim-Subscription-Key: YOUR_KEY" \
  "https://api.preflightapi.io/api/v1/\
metars/KJFK"

Returns parsed fields including flightCategory (VFR/MVFR/IFR/LIFR) as a go/no-go indicator. Collection endpoints wrap results in data + pagination.

04

Hobbyist vs enterprise

Pick by constraint, not by feature list

Weekend project

AviationAPI, then PreflightAPI

AviationAPI needs no signup at all and is permanently free — ideal for chart browsers and sim tooling. When you outgrow it, PreflightAPI's 5,000 free calls a month with no credit card is the most generous commercial free tier here by a factor of fifty.

Data analysis

BTS TranStats, unconditionally

Nothing else offers thirty-nine years of flight-level records for free with no key. Budget for ETL rather than API calls, and expect a two-to-three-month reporting lag.

Production, US operations

FAA directly, with PreflightAPI as a shortcut

For anything certified, the FAA's own CC0 surfaces are the only defensible source — both of the small providers explicitly disclaim operational use. PreflightAPI is a reasonable way to skip the fixed-width parsing while you build, not a system of record.

Global flight tracking

AviationStack, with reservations

It is the only one of the five with worldwide positions and schedules, so it wins by default — but 2/5 on Trustpilot, unnamed upstream vendors, a 1 req/min schedule cap on paid plans and repeated accuracy complaints mean you should evaluate alternatives such as OpenSky or FlightAware before committing.

05

Caveats worth reading first

Findings that change how you'd choose

  • 01Coverage barely overlaps. These are complements, not substitutes. Only AviationStack has global flight positions; only BTS has multi-decade history; only the FAA is authoritative. No single source covers both live and historical.
  • 02Two of five disclaim operational use. AviationAPI states it "was not intended for real-world use," and PreflightAPI notes it "has not been approved" by the FAA or AWC.
  • 03No one publishes an SLA or uptime figure. Not one of the five. Absence of complaints against the smaller providers reflects tiny user bases, not proven reliability.
  • 04AviationStack contradicts itself on the free tier — 100 requests a month on the live pricing page versus 500 in its archived official GitHub README. Its status page also failed to load during evaluation.
  • 05AviationAPI's docs were 429-throttled throughout testing, so its documented behaviour was confirmed against a 13 Feb 2026 archive snapshot and a mirrored Swagger file. Values may have drifted.
  • 06Licensing is unresolved for three of five. Only the FAA publishes a clear licence (CC0). BTS, AviationAPI and PreflightAPI publish no retrievable terms — PreflightAPI's /terms and /pricing pages block automated readers entirely.
  • 07FAA data formats are about to change. From the 3 September 2026 AIRAC cycle, NASR subscriber files alter both the legacy TXT and CSV layouts — budget parser maintenance if you ingest them.
